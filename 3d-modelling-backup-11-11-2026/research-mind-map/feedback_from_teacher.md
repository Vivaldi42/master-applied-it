Your document provides a useful initial overview of technologies for reconstructing 3D building environments. You demonstrate awareness of recent developments in computer vision and robotics, and you correctly distinguish between geometric reconstruction, visual scene representation, semantic mapping and structured building information models.

Your attention to hardware requirements and software licences is also valuable.

However, the document currently functions primarily as a technology inventory. To develop it into a convincing Master's-level research foundation, you need to strengthen the professional problem analysis, research methodology and connection with broader IT-domain knowledge.

1. Start from the maintenance problem, not the available technology

You explain that the organisation needs reliable building and asset information, but you do not yet investigate this need in sufficient detail. Identify concrete maintenance scenarios, the information professionals require and the limitations of current processes. Use these findings to establish requirements for the 3D models. This will provide a basis for evaluating which technologies are appropriate.

2. Make your technology exploration systematic

You have identified many interesting models and software frameworks, but the reasons for selecting them are not sufficiently transparent.

Document your literature search and establish criteria for comparing different approaches. Distinguish between methods that solve different problems, such as localisation, geometry reconstruction, semantic identification and building information modelling. Critically evaluate the strengths, limitations and assumptions of the selected approaches rather than primarily describing their functionality.

3. Strengthen the connection with robotics research

Your project offers an interesting opportunity to investigate how existing robotics methods can be applied in a different professional domain.

Develop a clearer understanding of the relevant concepts from robotics, particularly SLAM, mapping, localisation, sensor fusion and semantic mapping. Investigate which assumptions underlying these methods remain valid in building-maintenance applications and which additional requirements must be addressed.

4. Develop an explicit research methodology

Use the DOT Framework to explain how you will combine literature research, stakeholder investigation and technical experimentation. For each research question, identify the methods/methodology, expected evidence, evaluation criteria and limitations. The purpose is not simply to demonstrate that you have used different methods, but to create a transparent and credible basis for your decisions and conclusions.

5. Validate your findings through comparative experiments

Select a small number of promising approaches and compare them using representative input data.

Establish evaluation criteria before conducting the experiments. Pay particular attention to geometric accuracy, reconstruction completeness, robustness, computational performance and suitability for downstream asset modelling. Make your experiments reproducible by documenting datasets, configurations, model versions and evaluation procedures.

6. Define your intended contribution to the IT domain

At Master's level, your project should aim to produce knowledge beyond a working 3D model for one organisation.

Consider whether your research could contribute a reusable method-selection framework, an integration architecture or an evaluation approach for applying robotics-based reconstruction methods in building information systems. Define this intended contribution early and use it to guide your research.

7. Investigate the boundaries of your conclusions

A method that works well in one building or with one dataset may not perform equally well in other circumstances.

Investigate which environmental, technical and application-specific factors influence the results. Document these factors and use them to establish under which conditions your findings may be transferable.

Overall recommendation

Your technical exploration is a useful starting point, but the next step should not simply be to investigate more models.

Instead, strengthen the professional context analysis, develop a systematic research design and use targeted experiments to investigate the suitability of selected approaches.

Aim to transform the current technology inventory into an evidence-based investigation that produces both a practical solution and transferable knowledge about applying robotics-based 3D reconstruction methods in building-maintenance systems.
